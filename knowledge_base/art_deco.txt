TITLE: Art Deco Architecture

OVERVIEW

Art Deco architecture became prominent during the 1920s and 1930s. It represented modernity, luxury, technology, speed, progress, and optimism. The style appeared in skyscrapers, cinemas, hotels, apartment buildings, railway stations, and commercial buildings.

HISTORICAL BACKGROUND

Art Deco developed internationally during the interwar period. It was influenced by industrial design, ancient cultures, machinery, aviation, modern transportation, and decorative arts.

VISUAL CHARACTERISTICS

Art Deco buildings often use strong geometric forms, symmetry, vertical emphasis, stepped profiles, streamlined surfaces, and decorative patterns.

Common decorative motifs include zigzags, chevrons, sunbursts, fans, lightning shapes, fountains, geometric flowers, and repeated angular forms.

Skyscrapers may use setbacks that create a tiered or wedding-cake appearance. Vertical lines often make the building appear taller.

Later Art Deco developed into Streamline Moderne, which used rounded corners, horizontal bands, smooth surfaces, and forms inspired by ships, cars, and aircraft.

MATERIALS

Common materials include concrete, steel, glass, chrome, aluminium, polished stone, terracotta, decorative tiles, and metalwork.

STRUCTURAL FEATURES

Buildings usually have modern structural systems but are visually enriched with decorative geometry. Entrances, towers, window surrounds, and rooflines may receive special ornamentation.

COMMON BUILDING TYPES

Common building types include skyscrapers, cinemas, hotels, office buildings, apartment blocks, railway stations, theatres, and department stores.

FAMOUS EXAMPLES

Representative examples include the Chrysler Building, the Empire State Building, Rockefeller Center, the Palais de la Porte Dorée, and many historic cinemas and hotels.

HOW TO IDENTIFY IT

Art Deco can often be recognised by symmetry, geometric decoration, stepped forms, strong vertical lines, luxurious materials, stylised motifs, and elegant ornament.

HOW IT DIFFERS FROM SIMILAR STYLES

Art Deco differs from Bauhaus because Art Deco is more decorative, luxurious, symmetrical, and ornamental. Bauhaus is usually more functional, minimal, asymmetrical, and undecorated.
