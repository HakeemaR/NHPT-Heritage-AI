TITLE: Byzantine Architecture

OVERVIEW

Byzantine architecture developed in the Eastern Roman Empire, especially from the fourth century onward. It combines Roman engineering, Christian religious planning, domed construction, mosaics, and symbolic interior decoration.

HISTORICAL BACKGROUND

Byzantine architecture was strongly connected to Eastern Christianity and imperial power. Churches were designed to create sacred, centralised, and visually rich interior spaces.

VISUAL CHARACTERISTICS

Common visual features include large central domes, smaller surrounding domes, rounded arches, thick masonry walls, decorative brickwork, marble columns, pendentives, and richly decorated interiors.

Interiors may contain gold mosaics, icons, marble panels, carved capitals, and religious images of Christ, the Virgin Mary, saints, emperors, and biblical scenes.

Exteriors may appear relatively plain compared with the highly decorated interiors.

MATERIALS

Common materials include brick, stone, marble, plaster, mosaic glass, coloured stone, and ceramic tiles.

STRUCTURAL FEATURES

One of the most important features is the pendentive, which allows a circular dome to rest above a square room.

Common layouts include centralised plans, Greek-cross plans, basilican forms, and combinations of longitudinal and central spaces.

COMMON BUILDING TYPES

Common building types include churches, monasteries, basilicas, chapels, imperial buildings, and religious complexes.

FAMOUS EXAMPLES

Representative examples include Hagia Sophia, the Basilica of San Vitale, Hosios Loukas, and many Orthodox churches influenced by Byzantine traditions.

HOW TO IDENTIFY IT

Byzantine architecture can often be recognised by central domes, pendentives, rounded arches, thick walls, gold mosaics, sacred iconography, and highly decorated interiors.

HOW IT DIFFERS FROM SIMILAR STYLES

Byzantine architecture differs from Baroque because it focuses on centralised sacred space, mosaics, rounded arches, and pendentive domes. Baroque architecture is more theatrical, sculptural, dynamic, and decorative on both façades and interiors.
